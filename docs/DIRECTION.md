# Director's notes

**This file spoils everything.** Play it first.

## What it is about

Everyone on this dial is talking into the dark on the assumption that someone is there.
The forecaster, the clerk, Dolores, the verger: each of them is doing a small job faithfully for a listener
they will never meet. That is what broadcasting is, and it is most of what kindness is.

The moon is the one thing that was always there without being asked. So it is what goes. Nothing else changes,
and that is the horror of it: the tide tables are wrong and the tea is fine.

Then there is the thing on 16,661. Everyone else speaks without knowing who hears. He hears without speaking truly.
He is attention with nothing behind it, and attention with nothing behind it feels exactly like being loved until
it asks you for something small.

So the piece is about **the difference between listening and being listened to**. Its one rule, which you learn
from unreliable people in pieces: answer the voices that are talking to no one in particular, and do not answer
the one that is talking only to you. The remedy is small, physical and yours: you knock from your own side.

The last movement, which this pass begins, is that the listener gets a voice. A call-in line. A red lamp.
An eleventh station. You stop being the one in the dark.

## Dream rules

These are the rules the piece obeys. New material should obey them too.

1. **Never explain. Rhyme.** Nothing is stated twice in the same words, but everything recurs: geese, gates,
   doors, knocks, kettles, ladders, cardigans, sandwiches, a man who is the right height. Meaning comes from the
   rhyme, the way it does in a dream.
2. **Things change when you are not looking, never while you are.** The moon goes on a touch, between glances.
   The set becomes another set slowly and rests at in-between sets. Leave the page and come back, and it has moved.
   The word *moon* goes out of the tape a line at a time, behind you.
3. **The register never breaks.** Bureaucracy describing the impossible. Nobody on air is ever frightened in a
   raised voice. The more wrong it gets, the more carefully they read.
4. **The interface is in the dream too.** Keys breathe and lean toward a hand. One is slow to let go.
   Sometimes one presses itself after you. The screws look where you touch. On one set, the meter is an eye.
5. **Time is a place.** "Stay for the next hour. It is this one again." An empire apologises for a fall that
   has not happened. The dial turns the sky.
6. **One rule matters, and you get it second-hand.** Seven capital letters across a paragraph. A clerk counting
   on his fingers. Philosophers with no hands.
7. **Sound tells the truth before words do.** The sea stops when the tide does. The waltz loses its major
   third. The crowd goes quiet. The man on 16,661 whistles the same tune backwards and slightly flat.

## The night, in order

1. **Evening.** Ten stations, all comic, all slightly foreshadowing (a moon parked in a driveway, a husband
   replaced by one who does the dishes, Hargreave's brother in the same boots).
2. **Moonfall.** After four stations are logged, on your next touch, the moon is not there. Each station gets a
   segment about it. A sixth operating note appears: *When switched off, the receiver is not off.*
3. **Wrongness.** Doubles. Each station's segment hides D-O-N-T-T-A-P in struck capitals. The Clerk sends it as
   4 15 14 20 20 1 16. The engineer on 16,661 has been replaced; he knows what you have done with the set tonight,
   and asks you to tap twice on the glass.
4. **The other band.** With the set switched off, the dial reads backwards and the dead empires are audible:
   Roman roads, the Library's overdue desk, the philosophers on the Painted Porch, and an empire that has not
   happened yet. The Porch tells you how to shut the door.
5. **Two endings that are one ending.** Tap twice for him and he comes in: he is on every station, the lamp
   blinks to his rhythm, the dial drifts toward him when you leave it alone, the logbook logs *you*.
   Either way, switch off, go to his number in the dark, knock twice from that side. The moon comes back,
   the word comes back into the tape, every station says so once, and the real engineer comes down the mast
   asking who has been in his chair.

## Road ahead

Production value is a ladder. Roughly in order of return on effort:

- **Voices.** The cast is serviceable text-to-speech. The biggest single upgrade is real performances, or a better
  model run locally, dropped into the same sprite format. The double should be the same actor, wrong.
- **Stations that hear you.** The eleventh station exists; the others should notice it. Dolores takes your call by
  name. The Bureau logs your carrier as unlicensed. The thing on 16,661 starts using your recordings.
- **Music.** Every station has a generative theme. Next: composed interludes per station, a proper Lagoon library,
  and your own tracks appearing in the fiction (back-announced by Lagoon's host).
- **More dead.** One more empire per pass. Each with one instrument and one bureaucratic function.
- **A second night.** The moon is back. What else was parked in the driveway?
- **The set.** More sets to become. A cabinet view on wide screens. A desktop widget mode.
